## 1 | The one-minute version
This is the Ponte Vecchio, the Old Bridge. It is a medieval stone bridge over the Arno, and it is the only bridge in Florence that was spared from destruction in the Second World War.

The bridge has three segmental arches. Give yourself a minute to follow the line of the arches before you look at the shops.

## 2 | How old is it?
The present bridge has stood since about 1339 to 1345, and the standard date given for its rebuilding is 1345. The crossing itself is older: a bridge is believed to have first been built at this narrowest point of the river in Roman times, when the Via Cassia crossed here.

Floods have damaged it repeatedly. The bridge was severely damaged in the 1966 flood of the Arno.

## 3 | Who designed it?
Giorgio Vasari recorded the traditional view of his day that Taddeo Gaddi designed the bridge. This guide has not checked how firmly modern scholarship supports that attribution, so treat it as tradition.

The Torre dei Mannelli was built at the southeast corner of the bridge to defend it.

## 1 | Who works here now
Shops were once common on bridges, and the Ponte Vecchio is noted for the shops built along it. Butchers, tanners and farmers were the first tenants. Today the shops belong to jewellers, art dealers and souvenir sellers.

## 2 | Why shops on a bridge?
Building shops on bridges was once a common practice. The Ponte Vecchio is noted for the shops built along it.

## 1 | A fine for padlocks
Between 2005 and 2006 the city council removed 5,500 padlocks, known as love locks, from the railings around the bust of Cellini. According to the council, the padlocks were aesthetically displeasing and damaged the bust and its railings. There is now a fine for attaching love locks to the bridge.
